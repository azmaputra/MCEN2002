Tutorial 6.2  –  Square-Threaded Power Screw: Torque & Overhauling

Problem Statement

A single-start square-threaded power screw raises $W = 55.6$ kN. Mean pitch diameter $d_m = 25$ mm, pitch $p = 6$ mm, collar mean diameter $d_c = 38$ mm. Running friction $f = f_c = 0.1$.

(a) Find the major diameter.  (b) Estimate the starting torque to raise the load.  (c) If collar friction is eliminated, what thread coefficient of friction is needed to prevent overhauling?

Given answers:  (a) 28 mm   (b) 287.5 N·m   (c) 0.076

Solution

1
(a) Major diameter of the screw
Try it first, then reveal ↓
Step 1 Work up from the mean diameter using the thread depth

For a square thread the depth is $p/2$, and the mean (pitch) diameter sits one depth below the major diameter. So the major diameter is the mean diameter plus $p/2$:

$$d = d_m + \frac{p}{2} = 25 + \frac{6}{2} = 28\text{ mm}$$

$$d = 28\text{ mm}$$

Equivalently the minor (root) diameter is $d_r = d_m - p/2 = 22$ mm. The three diameters step down by $p/2 = 3$ mm each: major 28, mean 25, root 22.

2
(b) Starting torque to raise the load
Try it first, then reveal ↓
Step 2 Square-thread raising torque + collar, with static friction

The screw is single-start, so $L = p = 6$ mm. For a square thread $\alpha = 0$, so $\sec\alpha = 1$ and the formula is:

$$T_R = \frac{W d_m}{2}\left(\frac{L + \pi f d_m}{\pi d_m - f L}\right) + \frac{f_c W d_c}{2}$$

Starting torque uses static friction. With running $f = f_c = 0.1$, take $f_s = f_{c,s} = \tfrac{4}{3}(0.1) = 0.1333$.

Thread term:

$$T_{R,\text{thread}} = \frac{55\,600 \times 25}{2}\left(\frac{6 + \pi (0.1333)(25)}{\pi (25) - (0.1333)(6)}\right) = 695\,000 \times \frac{6 + 10.47}{78.54 - 0.80} = 695\,000 \times 0.2119 = 147.3\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

Collar term:

$$T_{R,\text{collar}} = \frac{0.1333 \times 55\,600 \times 38}{2} = 140.8\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
$$T_R = 147.3 + 140.8 = 288.1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

$$T_R \approx 288\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \quad(\text{book } 287.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m})$$

3
(c) Friction needed to prevent overhauling (no collar)
Try it first, then reveal ↓
Step 3 The self-locking threshold sets the minimum thread friction

With the collar removed, only the thread can hold the load. "Overhauling" is the load spinning the screw down by itself. To prevent it, the thread must be self-locking:

$$f\,\sec\alpha \; \ge \; \tan\lambda$$

For a square thread $\sec\alpha = 1$, and $\tan\lambda = \dfrac{L}{\pi d_m}$. The minimum coefficient is exactly at the boundary:

$$f_{\min} = \tan\lambda = \frac{L}{\pi d_m} = \frac{6}{\pi \times 25} = 0.0764$$

$$f_{\min} \approx 0.076$$

Physical reading: the helix angle here is $\lambda = \tan^{-1}(0.0764) = 4.37^\circ$. Any thread friction angle $\phi = \tan^{-1} f$ larger than $\lambda$ locks the screw. The actual $f = 0.1$ ($\phi = 5.71^\circ$) comfortably exceeds this, so with its collar the real jack is well self-locking; $0.076$ is just the bare-minimum thread friction if the collar's help disappears.

Common mistake 1: Confusing mean and major diameter. The torque uses $d_m = 25$ mm; the "major diameter" asked in (a) is $d_m + p/2 = 28$ mm. Feeding 28 mm into the torque formula overstates the answer.

Common mistake 2: Adding the collar to part (c). Overhauling is resisted by the thread alone once the collar is removed — that is the whole premise of the question, and it is why the answer reduces cleanly to $\tan\lambda$.

Common mistake 3: Using running friction for the starting torque in (b). Without the static bump ($\tfrac{4}{3}\times$) you get $T_R \approx 229$ N·m instead of $288$ N·m.
