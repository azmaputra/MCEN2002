Tutorial 6.5  –  Acme Screw Jack: Torque & Wrench Force

Problem Statement

A screw jack with a 25 mm single-start Acme screw (pitch $p = 10$ mm) raises $W = 20$ kN. A plain thrust collar of mean diameter $d_c = 40$ mm is used. Running friction $f = f_c = 0.1$.

(a) How much torque must be exerted to raise the load?  (b) What force is required at the end of a $0.6$ m wrench handle?

Given answers:  (a) $T = 98.47$ N·m   (b) $F = 164.1$ N

Solution

1
Screw geometry
Try it first, then reveal ↓
Step 1 Lead, mean diameter, helix angle

Single-start thread: $L = p = 10$ mm.

Diameter note (read carefully). To reproduce the textbook answer exactly, the mean diameter must be taken as $d_m = 25$ mm — i.e. the stated "25 mm" is treated as the mean diameter. If instead 25 mm is the major diameter, then $d_m = 25 - p/2 = 20$ mm and the torque works out to $T \approx 93.4$ N·m (not 98.47). This solution uses $d_m = 25$ mm to match the book; confirm from the original figure which diameter is intended.

$$d_m = 25\text{ mm} \qquad \lambda = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{L}{\pi d_m}\right) = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{10}{\pi \times 25}\right) = 7.26^\circ$$
2
(a) Torque to raise the load
Try it first, then reveal ↓
Step 2 Acme raising torque = thread + collar (running friction)

No starting torque is asked, so use running $f = f_c = 0.1$. Acme half-angle $\alpha = 14.5^\circ$, $\sec\alpha = 1.0329$:

$$T = \frac{W d_m}{2}\left(\frac{L + \pi f d_m \sec\alpha}{\pi d_m - f L \sec\alpha}\right) + \frac{f_c W d_c}{2}$$

Thread term:

$$T_{\text{thread}} = \frac{20\,000 \times 25}{2}\left(\frac{10 + \pi(0.1)(25)(1.0329)}{\pi(25) - (0.1)(10)(1.0329)}\right) = 250\,000 \times \frac{10 + 8.111}{78.54 - 1.033} = 250\,000 \times 0.2337 = 58.4\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

Collar term:

$$T_{\text{collar}} = \frac{0.1 \times 20\,000 \times 40}{2} = 40.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
$$T = 58.4 + 40.0 = 98.4\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

$$T \approx 98.4\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \quad(\text{book } 98.47\text{ N}\cdot\text{m})$$

3
(b) Force at the wrench handle
Try it first, then reveal ↓
Step 3 $F = T / R$

The wrench handle is $R = 0.6$ m long. Working in SI ($T = 98.4$ N·m):

$$F = \frac{T}{R} = \frac{98.4\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}{0.6\text{ m}} = 164\text{ N}$$

$$F \approx 164\text{ N} \quad(\text{book } 164.1\text{ N})$$

Force gain: 164 N at the wrench lifts 20 000 N — about a 120× advantage. The steep $p = 10$ mm lead makes this jack fast (10 mm rise per turn) but its helix angle $7.26^\circ$ vs friction angle $\tan^{-1}(0.1 \cdot \sec\alpha) = 5.9^\circ$ means the thread alone would overhaul; it relies on collar friction to hold.

Common mistake 1: The diameter ambiguity above — using $d_m = 20$ mm (if 25 mm were the major diameter) gives $T \approx 93.4$ N·m. The book's 98.47 N·m requires $d_m = 25$ mm. Always check the datum.

Common mistake 2: Forgetting the collar. Thread alone gives only $58.4$ N·m; the collar adds a full $40$ N·m.

Common mistake 3: Unit slips in part (b). Keep $T$ in N·m and $R$ in m, or $T$ in N·mm and $R$ in mm — never mix.
