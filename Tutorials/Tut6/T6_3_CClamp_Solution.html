Tutorial 6.3  –  C-Clamp: Handle Force for a Given Clamping Force

Problem Statement

An ordinary C-clamp uses a 13 mm Acme thread (pitch $p = 2.5$ mm, single start) and a collar of mean diameter $d_c = 16$ mm. Running friction $f = f_c = 0.15$. Estimate the force $F$ needed at the end of a 127 mm handle to develop a clamping force $W = 890$ N.

Given answer:   $F = 17.72$ N

Solution

1
Screw geometry
Try it first, then reveal ↓
Step 1 Lead, mean diameter, and helix angle

Single-start thread, so lead equals pitch: $L = p = 2.5$ mm. Acme mean diameter is the major diameter less one thread depth ($p/2$):

$$d_m = d - \frac{p}{2} = 13 - 1.25 = 11.75\text{ mm}$$
$$\lambda = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{L}{\pi d_m}\right) = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{2.5}{\pi \times 11.75}\right) = 3.88^\circ$$
2
Torque required to develop the clamping force
Try it first, then reveal ↓
Step 2 Acme "raising" torque = thread + collar (running friction)

Tightening the clamp is a "raising the load" action (advancing the screw against $W$). No starting torque is asked, so use the running coefficients $f = f_c = 0.15$ directly. With $\alpha = 14.5^\circ$, $\sec\alpha = 1.0329$:

$$T = \frac{W d_m}{2}\left(\frac{L + \pi f d_m \sec\alpha}{\pi d_m - f L \sec\alpha}\right) + \frac{f_c W d_c}{2}$$

Thread term:

$$T_{\text{thread}} = \frac{890 \times 11.75}{2}\left(\frac{2.5 + \pi(0.15)(11.75)(1.0329)}{\pi(11.75) - (0.15)(2.5)(1.0329)}\right) = 5228.8 \times \frac{2.5 + 5.719}{36.91 - 0.387} = 5228.8 \times 0.2251 = 1176.6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}$$

Collar term:

$$T_{\text{collar}} = \frac{0.15 \times 890 \times 16}{2} = 1068\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}$$
$$T = 1176.6 + 1068 = 2244.6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm} = 2.245\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

Thread and collar contribute almost equally here — the small screw and small collar are well matched. Note the torque is in N·mm because $d_m$, $d_c$, $L$ are all in mm.

3
Convert torque to handle force
Try it first, then reveal ↓
Step 3 $F = T / R$

The hand applies $F$ at radius $R = 127$ mm, producing torque $T = F R$. Solve for $F$:

$$F = \frac{T}{R} = \frac{2244.6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}}{127\text{ mm}} = 17.7\text{ N}$$

$$F \approx 17.7\text{ N} \quad(\text{book } 17.72\text{ N})$$

Mechanical advantage: a 17.7 N push (about the weight of a 1.8 kg bag) produces 890 N of clamping — a ~50× force gain. That is the value of the screw-plus-lever combination, bought at the cost of many turns and low efficiency.

Common mistake 1: Keeping torque in N·mm and handle length in m (or mixing units). Keep everything in mm, then $F$ comes out in N directly: N·mm ÷ mm = N.

Common mistake 2: Omitting the collar torque. Here it is nearly half the total; dropping it gives $F \approx 9$ N, badly low.

Common mistake 3: Using the 13 mm major diameter in the torque formula instead of $d_m = 11.75$ mm.
